TRANSPORT POLICY RESEARCH · BANGLADESH

Trip-reduction strategies in Bangladesh

A Bangladesh-focused review of common travel-demand strategies and the local conditions that influence whether those strategies are likely to work as intended.

Approach: literature synthesis · policy analysisTopics: pricing · transit · ride sharing · active travel
On this page
QuestionEvidenceArgumentLimitations

Research question

The project asked how well common demand-management measures transfer to Bangladesh when public-transport quality, road safety, enforcement, travel behaviour and institutional conditions differ from the settings in which many of those policies were first evaluated.

Evidence base

The work combined international strategy categories with Bangladesh-specific evidence on mode choice, ride-sharing interest, public-transport quality, parking, road capacity and enforcement. It was a policy and literature synthesis rather than an impact evaluation.

Main argument

Trip-reduction strategies need to be matched to the local mechanism that drives travel behaviour. Pricing, compact development, ride sharing and active travel can produce different effects depending on transit reliability, safety, access and trip purpose. The project therefore emphasized context rather than assuming that a policy label transfers unchanged between cities.

Limitations

The project relies on secondary evidence and does not estimate causal effect sizes. Bangladesh is also not one uniform travel market: large metropolitan areas, secondary cities and smaller municipalities have different transport supply, land-use patterns and traveller constraints.
