TRANSPORT POLICY RESEARCH · BANGLADESH

Trip-reduction strategies in Bangladesh

I reviewed common travel-demand strategies in a Bangladesh context, focusing on the local conditions that affect whether those strategies are likely to work as intended.

Approach: literature synthesis · policy analysisTopics: pricing · transit · ride sharing · active travel
On this page
QuestionEvidenceArgumentLimitations

Research question

I asked how well common demand-management measures transfer to Bangladesh when public-transport quality, road safety, enforcement, travel behaviour and institutional conditions differ from the settings in which many of those policies were first evaluated.

Evidence base

I combined international strategy categories with Bangladesh-specific evidence on mode choice, ride-sharing interest, public-transport quality, parking, road capacity and enforcement. This was a policy and literature synthesis, not an impact evaluation.

Main argument

My main conclusion was that trip-reduction strategies need to match the local mechanisms that shape travel behaviour. Pricing, compact development, ride sharing and active travel can have different effects depending on transit reliability, safety, access and trip purpose. I therefore focused on context instead of assuming that a policy label transfers unchanged between cities.

Limitations

This review relies on secondary evidence and does not estimate causal effect sizes. Bangladesh is also not one uniform travel market: large metropolitan areas, secondary cities and smaller municipalities have different transport supply, land-use patterns and traveller constraints.
