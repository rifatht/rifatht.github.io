Deciphering Traffic Dynamics: Analysing the Induced Traffic of the Padma Multipurpose Bridge

In this study, I estimate a present-day no-bridge counterfactual, compare it with observed bridge traffic, examine behavioural change and test long-term traffic scenarios under uncertainty.

Field: Transportation planning & geographyMethods: Counterfactual analysis · GIS · qualitative interviews · Monte Carlo simulationSupervisor: Professor Dr. Md Ahsanul Kabir

Research problem

The Padma Bridge replaced a ferry- and launch-dependent crossing system with a fixed road connection. Post-opening traffic rose sharply, but I do not treat all observed bridge traffic as induced travel. Some movement reflects ordinary growth, route diversion, mode transfer and previously suppressed demand. I first estimate what cross-Padma traffic would likely have looked like without the bridge, then interpret the difference.

Mixed-methods counterfactual design

I combine temporal and spatial counterfactual reasoning. Pre-bridge movement trends provide the main temporal no-bridge estimate, while district-level built-up area and census information support spatial interpretation and sensitivity checks. I use Monte Carlo simulation to represent uncertainty instead of treating the counterfactual as a single known value. Interviews help me distinguish reported additional travel from destination change, mode transfer and route diversion.

I describe the numerical difference as bridge-attributable excess traffic, not pure induced traffic. The distinction matters because the counterfactual allows ordinary pre-bridge movement to grow, while the remaining excess can still include newly generated travel and redistributed movement.

Behavioural interpretation

Respondents reported increased trip frequency, newly feasible trips, destination changes, mode transfer and route diversion. I treat frequency increases and newly feasible trips as the clearest behavioural evidence of additional travel. Route and mode shifts are why I do not label the entire excess as induced demand.

Thirty-year scenario forecast

I use scenario-based forecasting rather than a single deterministic prediction. Under alternative assumptions about the persistence of induced demand, the 2054 traffic range is approximately 33,169 to 44,537 vehicles per day. I use that range for planning interpretation and sensitivity testing, not as an exact prediction of future traffic.

Interpretation and limitations

The present no-bridge condition is unobservable, so my result depends on the counterfactual assumptions and the quality of historical movement, spatial and traffic data. Built-up area is a supporting proxy, not direct proof of travel generation, and the interviews help interpret mechanisms rather than decompose every vehicle at population level. I therefore keep bridge-attributable excess traffic separate from pure induced demand and report the uncertainty around the estimate.
