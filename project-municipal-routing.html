MUNICIPAL PLANNING COURSEWORK · SOLID-WASTE ROUTING

Municipal solid-waste collection and routing

In this municipal-services project, I examined the spatial relationship between waste-generating points, transfer locations, vehicle movement and the final disposal route.

Methods: GIS routing · service-network analysisContext: municipal solid-waste collection
On this page
Collection systemRoutingPlanning useLimitations

Collection system

I represented a three-stage municipal collection system linking waste-generating points to primary collection or dustbin locations, secondary transfer stations and a final treatment or disposal location.

Routing analysis

I used GIS network analysis to examine how collection vehicles could move between transfer locations and the final disposal site. This connected service planning with road-network constraints and the spatial distribution of collection points.

Planning use

I used the routing analysis to organize municipal service areas, compare route lengths and examine how transfer locations and road-network structure affect collection efficiency.

Limitations

A full operational model would require reliable waste quantities, vehicle capacities, loading and unloading times, shift constraints, road restrictions and time-dependent travel conditions. I treat this as a spatial routing and service-network exercise rather than a full operations model.
