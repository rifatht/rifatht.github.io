Cyclone-induced flood vulnerability in coastal Bangladesh

During my internship with UNDP’s Sundarbans Area Based Programme, I developed a remote-sensing workflow to examine cyclone-related flooding across Khulna, Satkhira and Bagerhat using Landsat-derived environmental indicators and Random Forest regression.

Study context: Cyclones Sidr & AilaMethods: Landsat indices · Random Forest regressionRegion: Khulna · Satkhira · Bagerhat

Research question

I tested whether satellite-derived environmental conditions could model cyclone-induced flood vulnerability across southwest coastal Bangladesh. Cyclone Sidr provided the main historical training context, and Cyclone Aila provided a second event for testing how well the relationship transferred.

Inputs and model

I combined Landsat 5, 8 and 9 imagery with GNDVI, NDVI, NDWI, land-surface temperature, salinity index and simple-ratio indicators. I used temporal change in NDWI as a proxy for flood intensity and Random Forest regression to model nonlinear relationships between the environmental indicators and the flood signal.

Reported model performance

R² 0.83Sidr scenario
R² 0.79Aila scenario

I also used MAE and RMSE for model evaluation. The output was a set of spatial predictions that translated the remote-sensing variables into mapped flood-vulnerability patterns for disaster-risk and resilience planning.

Current methodological extension

I am revisiting this research to strengthen temporal validation, update the geospatial inputs and develop a broader vulnerability framework that can incorporate land use, infrastructure and localized resilience factors.

Limits

The main limits are the small number of historical event contexts, differences between satellite acquisitions and the use of remote-sensing proxies for flood conditions. I treat the model outputs as spatial evidence within those data limits, not as a complete representation of social and infrastructure vulnerability.
