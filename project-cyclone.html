Cyclone-induced flood vulnerability in coastal Bangladesh

During my internship with UNDP’s Sundarbans Area Based Programme, I developed a remote-sensing workflow to examine cyclone-related flooding across Khulna, Satkhira and Bagerhat using Landsat-derived environmental indicators and Random Forest regression.

Research question

The study asked whether satellite-derived environmental conditions could be used to model cyclone-induced flood vulnerability across the southwest coastal region of Bangladesh. Cyclone Sidr provided the main historical training context and Cyclone Aila provided a second event for testing how well the relationship transferred.

Inputs and model

The workflow combined Landsat 5, 8 and 9 imagery with GNDVI, NDVI, NDWI, land-surface temperature, salinity index and simple-ratio indicators. Temporal change in NDWI was used as a proxy for flood intensity, and Random Forest regression was used to model nonlinear relationships between environmental indicators and the flood signal.

The project also used MAE and RMSE for model evaluation. The practical output was a set of spatial predictions that translated remote-sensing variables into mapped flood-vulnerability patterns for disaster-risk and resilience planning.

Current methodological extension

I am revisiting this research to strengthen temporal validation, update the geospatial inputs and develop a broader vulnerability framework that can incorporate land use, infrastructure and localized resilience factors.

Limits

The study is constrained by the number of historical event contexts, differences between satellite acquisitions and the use of remote-sensing proxies for flood conditions. The model outputs should therefore be interpreted as spatial evidence within those data limits rather than as a complete representation of social and infrastructure vulnerability.
