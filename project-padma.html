Predicting the Influence of the Padma Bridge on Future Urban Growth

My undergraduate thesis combined multi-date Landsat imagery, Random Forest land classification, spatial predictors, Markov transition modelling and Cellular Automata to examine possible urban-growth patterns associated with the Padma Bridge.

Field: Urban growth & infrastructureMethods: Remote sensing · Random Forest · Markov + CASoftware: R · ArcGIS · Dinamica EGO

Research question

I examined how improved connectivity from the Padma Bridge may influence future urban development. Because a long post-construction land-use record was not yet available, I used the Jamuna Bridge region as an analogue for exploring infrastructure-associated growth patterns.

Data and land classification

I used ten Landsat scenes across the Padma and Jamuna study regions and compared multiple classification approaches before choosing Random Forest for the main land-cover series. The spatial predictors represented accessibility, network structure, terrain and development constraints.

For the growth model, I used Markov transition logic, weights of evidence and Cellular Automata patch and expander functions in Dinamica EGO. I focused on the spatial pattern of future development rather than simply extrapolating total urban area.

Key outputs

My final scenario showed uneven growth pressure across the study area, with stronger projected influence in some upazilas than others. I use that spatial variation to identify where land management, infrastructure and service demand may change most rapidly.

Limitations

This is a scenario-based spatial model, not a causal estimate of the bridge effect. The analogue approach depends on how comparable the two bridge regions are, and land-cover classification and transition assumptions add uncertainty. I therefore treat the forecasts as planning scenarios, not exact predictions.
